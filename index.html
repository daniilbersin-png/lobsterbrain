A ganglion, a stomach, and no controller

The circuit — the pyloric network of the lobster's stomatogastric ganglion, fourteen neurons whose every synapse was found with paired electrodes and has been redrawn by three generations of hands since — run as the conductance model of Prinz, Bucher & Marder 2004, in this browser, right now. Between the model and a drawing of the stomach a fixed arithmetic: a cell's firing becomes a muscle's pull, a dilator opens the pylorus, a constrictor closes it. Nothing written by anyone decides the order. Either the wiring makes the rhythm or it does not, and either answer is reported with its numbers.

the stomach · a schematic, not a body

A stomach that is drawn, and a valve that only proves the wires

The lobster chews inside its stomach. Three teeth in the gastric mill grind under one rhythm; behind them the pylorus filters and pumps under the other, the one running above. The stomatogastric ganglion sits on the stomach's roof, inside the ophthalmic artery, and most of its thirty cells are motor neurons to those muscles. No physical model of any of this exists. What is shown is a drawing whose pyloric valve opens when the model's PD fires and closes when LP and PY do; it proves that the model reaches the drawing, and nothing else.

kept off the page

Three things the animal does that this model cannot

Each is documented in the literature and absent here on purpose, because adding it would mean choosing numbers this page has no measured source for.

a
NeuromodulationThe ganglion is bathed by blood carrying more than twenty modulators — dopamine, serotonin, octopamine, proctolin, RPCH, CCAP and others — and each reconfigures the same cells into a different rhythm; that is the central lesson of forty years of work on this circuit (Marder & Bucher 2007). The model has no modulator, so it has one rhythm and only one.
not modelled
b
TemperatureIn the crab's copy of this circuit the period roughly halves between 7 and 23 °C while the phases hold still (Tang et al. 2010). The model runs at whatever temperature its kinetics were measured at and cannot be warmed.
not modelled
c
Next: the gastric millThe other rhythm of the same ganglion, the one that moves the teeth: LG, MG, DG, the four GMs and Int1, with a period of five to ten seconds. Its wiring is known too. Not yet modelled.

what is measured and what is chosen

Two lists, kept apart

The page is its own result file; this is the short form of what it separates.

  • Measured, from the literature. The circuit's membership and the sign of every chemical synapse in it, all inhibitory. The order of the rhythm and the range of its period in the animal. The cells' current kinetics, from voltage clamp of cultured lobster stomatogastric neurons (Liu et al. 1998, after Turrigiano et al. 1995).
  • Chosen. Three model cells for fourteen: AB and the two PDs as one, the PY cells as one, VD and IC left out, as Prinz et al. 2004 did. One conductance set per cell, transcribed from that paper's candidates; the transcription is in the source, and any slip in it is this page's, not theirs. The seven synaptic strengths: a few hundred combinations of the values Prinz et al. used, 3 to 100 nS, searched in a browser against the phases Bucher et al. 2005 reported, and the closest kept. The pass rule. The 0.05 ms step. The arithmetic that turns a rate into a pull and a pull into the drawing's gap.
  • Not possible from the literature. A lobster connectome; a count of the lobster's neurons — estimates exist, counts do not; the modulatory state of any particular animal at any particular moment.
what the model is not

Any one of these could make or abolish the rhythm

None of them is the measured wiring. Each is a simplification the page inherits from the published model or adds on its own, and the result file — this page — carries the same list.

  • Three cells for fourteen. AB and the two PDs are one cell, so their gap junctions do not exist; the five to eight PYs are one cell; VD and IC are absent, and so is everything they do.
  • Graded synapses only. Transmission follows the presynaptic voltage through a sigmoid at −35 mV; there are no spike-mediated synapses, and every synapse of one type has the same reversal, −70 mV glutamatergic, −80 mV cholinergic.
  • Identical kinetics. One set of eight currents for all three cells — INa, ICaT, ICaS, IA, IKCa, IKd, IH and a leak — differing only in their maximal conductances. Fitted to cultured cells, which are not the cells in the ganglion.
  • No modulators, no temperature, no gastric mill. The circuit that in the animal is never without them runs here without any of them.
  • A stomach with no muscles. The drawing's pull is a low-passed rate, with no length–tension curve, no load and no food, and the valve gap is one line of arithmetic on it.

what it is

A real circuit, not a metaphor

Every connection in the pyloric circuit was found with two glass electrodes in the stomach ganglion of an actual lobster, one pair of cells at a time, and has been checked by every lab that has worked on it since. Nothing is inferred from a stain, nothing is sampled from a distribution, and it is not a neural network “inspired by” a brain.

The stomatogastric ganglion of Homarus americanus holds about thirty neurons, most of them motor neurons to the stomach's muscles, and two circuits in one: the pyloric network of fourteen cells that runs at about a cycle a second, and the gastric mill network that moves the teeth once every five to ten seconds. It is the circuit on which the field learned that a wiring diagram is not enough — the same cells, under different modulators, produce different rhythms — and it is, by common consent, the best-understood circuit of any animal. The cells here are the Liu, Golowasch, Abbott & Marder 1998 model; the circuit is the three-cell reduction of Prinz, Bucher & Marder 2004; the comparison numbers are from Bucher, Prinz & Marder 2005.

before this

The lobster was mapped first

Decades before anyone sliced a fly into electron-microscope sections, the lobster's stomach circuit was drawn by hand from paired recordings — Maynard 1972, Mulloney & Selverston 1974, Selverston & Moulins 1987 — and it has been the reference circuit of neuroscience ever since.

This site follows the fly's. That one wired a measured nervous system to a measured body and reported that, under a model that simple, the body did not move. This one starts from the other end: a circuit small enough to be known completely, whose whole job is one rhythm, and asks whether the wiring alone makes it. So far the answer is yes, in the numbers above, with every caveat beside them.

what is not real, stated plainly

The parts that aren't the lobster

  • The stomach does not chew on this page. Nothing here shows a lobster's stomach moving, because there is no stomach: the drawing is a schematic driven by three rates, and the teeth in it do not move at all. If a physical model ever exists, the numbers will say what it did.
  • It cannot read. A stomach ganglion has no language and no plan. It did not choose a word on this page, did not form a view about anything, and is not steering anything. Nothing it does is a message.
  • The voice is a narrator. Every post on X in the lobster's name is written by a language model that is handed the page's telemetry and asked to write in the first person. Every number in a draft is checked against that packet before it goes out, and a draft with a number that is not in the packet is thrown away. The circuit is real; the words, and any “appetite” in them, are the narrator's.
  • Measured and chosen are kept apart. The wiring, its signs and the animal's phases are read from the papers. Which cells stand in for which, how strong each synapse is, how a rate becomes a pull, and what counts as a pass were decided by a person and written down before the page ran. The lists above are the short form; the source has all of it.
  • The rhythm is live; the comparisons are copied. Every number in the rhythm board is measured from the model as it runs in your browser and will differ from another machine's only in the last digit and the moment of reading. The animal's phases and everything else from the literature were typed in as the papers stood on 2026-09-13, and the page polls nothing but the chain.
